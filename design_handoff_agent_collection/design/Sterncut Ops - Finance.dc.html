F4The week · a run that can be released, and a statement that closes

The money rail existed and the period did not, so nothing could be shown to an owner and nothing could be released. Four decisions are baked into this layout.

A run is a draft that gets released, not a thing agents build as they go. The alternative was tempting — agents collect through the week, the run assembles itself — but then the second total does not exist until Thursday, and the second total is the only thing that answers the question Karima actually has on Friday: is Sterncut collecting this week or paying? A draft can be read whole before anyone presses anything; a run that grows cannot. Cost of the choice: a shop that goes live mid-week has no line at all (FIN-14, Nord Barbier), and that has to be said in words rather than left as a missing row.

Exclusions get sentences, not a count. “38 of 42” is the number that gets a run released by mistake. Four shops are out for four unrelated reasons, and one of them — Barbier Mesnana, suspended — is money we owe and are holding, which is the sentence most likely to become a theft accusation if it is left implicit. So it carries the amount, the date it unlocks, and the name of the person who tells the owner.

The header equals the sum of the lines. FIN-16 answers the 1 566 / 1 514 question by refusing the premise: 1 566 DH is what crosses the counter and is the only number the owner is asked to trust; 1 514 DH earns its place only as a named subtotal — this week’s movement — because a carried line follows it. A difference with no line under it is a bug, not a rounding.

The clock runs from the oldest uncollected dirham. Measuring from the previous collection would restart the 14 days every time a barber takes a cash top-up, and Le Fade takes one most days — a daily top-up shop could hold our money forever and never be late. So the age of a shop’s float is the age of its oldest unsettled movement, which makes Le Fade day 6 of 14 on Friday, not day 0.

Rejected: a minus sign. The owing direction (OSH-17) is a different sentence with a different action — have cash ready in the till by Friday evening — not the paid layout with a sign flipped. Also rejected: editing a released week to fix a late refund (FIN-17), which would make a document we already handed over disagree with itself.

Not drawn, on purpose: no fee or commission line anywhere in this run — v1 charges nothing, and the run moves exactly two kinds of money past each other. No prepaid passes. No RIB, no batch file, no account-name matching: releasing a run dispatches agent visits, not a bank instruction. Standing contradictions: FIN-01 still shows an 8% “our fee” column, a bank batch and a missing-RIB exclusion, and FIN-02 prices commission — both are superseded by FIN-14–15 and should be cut or rebuilt, not read.
Tables the backend does not have: settlement_run (week, state draft | released | closed, released_at, released_by) and settlement_line (run, shop, direction, amount, visit state) — the run being a draft is what makes those two rows rather than one; statement_item (line, kind, booking_ref, occurred_at, amount, source_week) append-only, where source_week is what lets a carried correction name where it came from; and a per-shop oldest_uncollected_at, which no current float table can answer. Missing state: there is no dispute anywhere in the product, so “I disagree with this deduction” can only open a support case (SUP-05) that ends in another line next week. I have said that on the screen instead of inventing a dispute flow.

F3Slice 3 · the one amount netting cannot carry

Netting works because it moves one number between two businesses. A tip is owed to a named man with no account, so it rides inside the shop's Friday number and is then handed over by someone who is not entitled to it. FIN-13 is built around that: received and earmarked must always match to the dirham because Sterncut takes nothing, and barber confirmation is the only evidence the money finished its journey — no bank line can prove it. The pattern it exists to catch is on screen: two barbers at one shop, 40 DH short between them, in the same week. The recommended remedy is to pay the shortfall out of Sterncut's own pocket, because arguing costs more — withholding a shop's whole net over 20 DH is not on the screen.

B4Sterncut paying the shops — the money the product has never shown

Build plan block 4, item 79. Every settlement screen so far has been a shop paying a barber — BER-04, ORP-01, BKN-05. Nothing showed Sterncut paying the shops, which is the transaction the whole business rests on.

Two positions worth arguing. First, held is not forfeited (FIN-01): Le Fade Tanger is suspended and its 6 661 DH is held, not kept — a platform that quietly keeps a suspended shop’s earnings is stealing with a policy on top, so the amount and its unlock date show on the owner’s own reports. Second, walk-ins through the queue are charged 0% (FIN-02): Sterncut did not bring that customer through the door, and charging for the privilege of logging him would make the queue — the one feature that gets shops onto the platform at all — something they route around.

FIN-03 exists mainly to hold one line: wallet balances are a liability, not revenue. Counting customer money as income inflates the top line and generates a tax bill on money we may have to hand back.
