T8Slice 2 · the Friday number, in both directions

The owner’s side of FIN-16. One number per week, and it can point either way: OSH-16 is the week Sterncut hands Youssef cash, OSH-17 is the week he hands cash back.

The owing direction is not a minus sign on the paid layout. It is a different sentence, a different colour and a different action — nothing to receive and sign for, but an amount to have ready in the till before Friday evening. It also needs a sentence the paid screen does not: this is not a bill. Sterncut charges nothing; the money going back is our own float, which his barbers took over the counter as wallet top-ups. Getting that wrong turns a bookkeeping event into a fee, and a fee he never agreed to.

Every deduction carries a reference and a time. Four cash top-ups with the barber’s name and the minute he took the money, and a refund with the booking, the cut time and the refund time. A smaller number at the top with no lines under it is what makes an owner call the office; the point of the lines is that he can check any one of them against his own day.

The total is the sum of the lines, always. 1 566 DH is what he puts on the counter. 1 514 DH is labelled this week and exists only because a carried line follows it — see OSH-18, where a refund issued after week 35 had already been paid out comes back as its own line rather than as an edit to a week he has already counted in his hand.

Not drawn: no Sterncut fee or commission line on either statement — v1 charges nothing, and the whole design rests on that being visibly true. No pass balances. No bank or RIB anywhere: both directions are an agent, a till and a signature. Missing state: the “this isn’t right” button on OSH-18 opens a support case, because the product has no dispute state on a statement line. That is what is drawn; a real dispute needs a state on the line, a rule about who decides, and a message when it closes.

T7Slice 3 · what my shop pays Sterncut

The owner's side of SET-17. The whole design problem is the waived month: a bill for 0 DH that must not read as a bill, and must not read as free forever either. So OSH-14 shows the real 490 DH struck through beside the 0 DH, names the date it starts (1 October, his own, not a platform date), and says plainly that the three free months are never owed later. OSH-15 is the same arithmetic once it bites — and the thing it has to prove is that nothing new crosses the counter: the whole charge lands in one of the four Friday nets he already collects — the last of the month — not a second payment. Both screens repeat one sentence, because it is the one an owner will not take on trust: Sterncut never touches the cash handed over in the chair.

T6Slice 2 · the owner sets his own deposit

T3When it breaks in the shop

The barber's failures are not the customer's. He's mid-cut, one-handed, with someone in the chair and three waiting — so the rule from Customer SYS-08 gets sharper here: an error must never stop the queue moving. Every screen keeps the chair working offline and syncs later; none of them asks him to stop and fix something while a client watches. Six states — connection, double-booking, a failed collection, an expiring licence, a suspended shop, and a stale app.

T4Two switches that were only pretending

Both of these are columns the code writes and the UI never reflects. salons.accepting_bookings is written by the header power button and read by nothing — so the owner thinks he closed the shop and requests keep landing on his barbers. salons.float_cap_cents is enforced in SQL with no screen, which means the stop lands while a customer's cash is already in the barber's hand. A switch has to say what it actually does, and a limit has to warn before it bites. OSH-09–BTD-07 are the pause; BCF-06–BCF-07 are the cap.
